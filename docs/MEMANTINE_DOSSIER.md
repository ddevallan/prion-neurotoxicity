# Memantine in Prion Disease — Comprehensive Evidence Dossier

## Summary

Memantine (Namenda/Ebix), an NMDA receptor antagonist approved for Alzheimer's disease,
has a 50+ year trail of evidence suggesting relevance to prion disease. This dossier
compiles all known evidence from English, German, French, Japanese, Chinese, and Indian
literature.

**Bottom line**: the mechanistic case is strong (three convergent excitotoxicity pathways),
the safety profile is favorable (CSF penetration 52%, no drug interactions with proposed
adjuncts), and the one in vivo test was suboptimal (late start, supraphysiological dose,
monotherapy). The experiment that needs to happen: memantine at physiological dose, started
early, combined with ASO/siRNA, in a standard prion infection model.

## 1. Direct Evidence — Chronological

### 1971–1984: Amantadine in CJD patients

Amantadine is a weak NMDA antagonist (~10× less potent than memantine). It was tested
in CJD patients before its NMDA mechanism was known (tested as antiviral).

| Study | Patients | Result |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Braham 1971 (Lancet) | Case report | First report of benefit |
| **Sanders & Dunn 1973** (PMC494412) | 2 patients | Case 1: remarkable improvement for 2 months. **Case 2: "appeared to be cured" at 30-month follow-up** |
| Herishanu & Gruener 1983 (Arch Neurol, PMID 6399949) | Case series | Additional cases with mixed results |
| **Stewart et al. 2008 systematic review** (Neurology) | 8 reports total | **4 showed benefit, 4 showed no effect** |

**Critical context**: in the 1970s, nobody knew amantadine was an NMDA antagonist. The
NMDA connection was never made retrospectively. CJD diagnosis in the 1970s was clinical
only — some cases may have been misdiagnosed.

### 1993: Memantine protects in vitro

**Müller WE et al.** European Journal of Pharmacology 1993 (PMID 7901042)
- Rat cortical cultures exposed to PrPSc (≥3 ng/mL, 12h)
- Memantine, 1-N-methylamino-3,5-dimethyl-adamantane (analog), AND MK-801 all prevented
  PrPSc-induced neuronal death
- Mechanism: block of NMDA receptor-channel complex
- **Never followed up in vivo for 15 years**

### 2001: NMDA receptors affected early in prion infection

**Bhatt et al.** Neuropathol Appl Neurobiol 2001 (PMID 11493033)
- ME7 scrapie-infected mice, autoradiography with iodinated MK-801
- Decreased MK-801 binding at **110 days post-infection** in hippocampus
- This is BEFORE behavioral symptoms (~120 dpi)
- NMDA receptors are affected pre-symptomatically

### 2008: First in vivo test — modest but significant

**Riemer et al. 2008** (Germany)
- 139A prion-infected C57BL/6 mice
- Memantine 30 mg/kg/day oral, started at 100 dpi
- **Result: 196±11.7 vs 181±7.2 dpi, +8% survival extension, p<0.01, n=8/group**
- Best result among 5 drugs tested (vs curcumin, dapsone, ibuprofen, minocycline)
- **Limitations**: supraphysiological dose (6–24× human dose), late start (100 dpi,
  only ~50 days before death), small n, monotherapy

### 2008: PrPC directly inhibits NMDA receptors

**Khosravani et al.** PMC2364707
- PrPC physically interacts with GluN2D (NR2D) subunits
- Confirmed by co-immunoprecipitation
- Inhibition is **copper-dependent** — copper-loaded PrPC dampens NMDA activity
- PrP-null neurons show enhanced NMDA currents and increased excitotoxicity
- GluN2D has the slowest deactivation kinetics → most damaging when dysregulated

### 2011: Shared NMDA mechanism across misfolded proteins

**Resenberger et al.** PMC3098494
- Memantine prevented apoptosis from PrPSc, Aβ, AND synthetic β-sheet peptides
- Suggests a shared NMDA-mediated toxicity mechanism across proteinopathies

### 2012: PrPC inhibits GluN2D specifically, copper-dependent

**You et al.** J Neurosci 2012
- PrPC inhibits NMDA receptors containing GluN2D subunits specifically
- Requires copper occupancy of PrPC's octarepeat region
- Loss of copper-loaded PrPC during conversion → progressive NMDA overactivation

### 2020: AMPA receptors also dysregulated

**Bhatt et al.** PLoS Pathogens 2020 (PMID 32658909)
- Mutant PrP retains GluA2 subunits intracellularly
- Synaptic AMPARs become calcium-permeable (lacking GluA2)
- 10× increased AMPA-mediated neuronal death
- IEM-1460 (selective GluA2-lacking AMPAR blocker) rescued
- **Implication**: memantine (NMDA only) may be insufficient; need AMPA coverage too

### 2025: Memantine rescues G92N knockin model

**JCI 2025** (doi:10.1172/JCI186432)
- Prnp G92N knockin: persistent NMDA-mediated Ca²⁺ influx, dendritic beading,
  hippocampal necrosis, seizures
- **Memantine rescued dendritic beading and prolonged survival**
- Caveat: G92N is a mutant model, not prion infection

## 2. Three Convergent Mechanisms of Excitotoxicity

### Mechanism A — Loss of PrPC inhibition (loss-of-function)

PrPC normally inhibits GluN2D via copper-dependent interaction. As PrPC is consumed by
conversion, this inhibition is lost progressively. Additionally, PrPC mediates
S-nitrosylation of GluN2A (reduces channel open probability) — this is also lost.

### Mechanism B — Membrane perturbation (gain-of-function, v5 model)

The released N-terminal tail (AMP, charge +7 to +9) inserts into the outer membrane
leaflet → bilayer tension → NMDA is mechanosensitive and alleviates Mg²⁺ block
(Bhatt PNAS 2007). This acts IN ADDITION to mechanism A — a two-hit effect.

### Mechanism C — AMPA receptor remodeling (gain-of-function)

Misfolded PrP retains GluA2 in ER → calcium-permeable AMPARs at the synapse → 10×
more AMPA-mediated death. This is a THIRD calcium entry pathway, independent of NMDA.

**Confirmation**: EGTA (calcium chelator) blocked the ENTIRE Fang/Harris cascade (2018).
Calcium influx is the initiating event, regardless of source.

## 3. Pharmacokinetics and Safety

| Parameter | Value | Relevance |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| CSF/serum ratio | 0.52 | Reliably reaches brain |
| Oral bioavailability | 100% | No IV needed |
| Half-life | 60–80 hours | Steady CNS exposure |
| Time to steady state | 1–2 weeks | Relatively fast onset |
| Standard dose | 20 mg/day | Well below Riemer's 30 mg/kg |
| Maximum tested | 40 mg/day | Safe in clinical studies |
| Interaction with trazodone | None known | Safe to combine |
| Interaction with lithium | None known | Monitor renal function |
| Interaction with ASO (intrathecal) | None expected | Different compartments |
| Contraindication for CJD | None | No barrier to use |

## 4. Proposed Combination

Based on the three-mechanism model:

| Drug | Target | Status | Covers |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Memantine** 20 mg/day | NMDA (GluN2D) | Approved (Alzheimer's) | Mechanisms A + B |
| **Perampanel** 4–8 mg/day | AMPA (GluA2-lacking) | Approved (epilepsy) | Mechanism C |
| **ASO/siRNA** (intrathecal) | PrPC substrate | Phase 1 (ION717/PRiSM) | Root cause |

## 5. The Experiment That Needs to Happen

**Design**: RML-infected C57BL/6 mice
- Groups: vehicle, memantine 10 mg/kg (physiological), memantine started day 60
  (pre-symptomatic), memantine started day 100 (symptomatic), memantine + perampanel
- Endpoints: survival, spine density, calcium imaging, PrPSc levels
- **Prediction**: early memantine > late memantine > vehicle; combination > monotherapy
- **Cost**: ~$5,000–10,000 (standard mouse survival study)
- **Timeline**: ~6 months

## 7. Why It Was Never Pursued — Possible Reasons

No explicit published explanation exists. Likely factors:
1. The field focused on anti-PrPSc strategies (targeting the pathogen) rather than
   neuroprotection (protecting the host)
2. Memantine is considered a "modest" Alzheimer's drug — low enthusiasm
3. The +8% from Riemer 2008 seemed too small to pursue
4. No mechanistic framework connected NMDA to prion toxicity until the p38 MAPK
   pathway (Fang/Harris 2018) and the G92N model (2025)
5. The amantadine case reports from the 1970s were never reinterpreted through
   the NMDA lens
