# Therapeutic Landscape for Prion Disease (August 2026)

Comprehensive mapping of current, preclinical, failed, and proposed therapeutic approaches,
evaluated against the v5 model of prion neurotoxicity.

---

## Active Clinical Trials

### ION717 — Antisense Oligonucleotide (Ionis Pharmaceuticals)

- **Mechanism:** Intrathecal ASO targeting PRNP mRNA → degrades transcript → reduces PrPC substrate.
- **Status:** Phase 1/2a (PrProfile trial). 56 symptomatic patients enrolled. Third dosing cohort added March 2026 based on encouraging safety and target engagement. Primary completion pushed to February 2027.
- **Preclinical:** <25% PrP suppression sufficient for prophylactic benefit; post-symptomatic dosing gave 2–4× survival extension in mice.
- **v5 verdict:** Correct target — removes substrate, stops the cascade at source.
- **Sponsor:** Ionis Pharmaceuticals.
- **References:** Minikel et al., Nucleic Acids Res 2020;48:10615–10631. CureFFI update March 2026. Prion Alliance mid-year 2026.

### PRiSM — Divalent siRNA (Broad Institute / UMass Chan / Regeneron)

- **Mechanism:** Two linked siRNAs silence PRNP mRNA; broader CNS distribution than single siRNA; intracerebroventricular delivery.
- **Status:** Phase 1. FDA IND cleared March 2025. Enrolling 15 symptomatic patients as of April 2026.
- **Preclinical:** Single ICV dose → 49% reduction in brain PrP, 64% survival extension even in symptomatic mice.
- **v5 verdict:** Correct target — same rationale as ION717 but potentially broader CNS distribution.
- **Sponsor:** Broad Institute, UMass Chan, Regeneron.
- **References:** Broad Institute news 2026. bioRxiv 2024. CureFFI update April 2026.

## Preclinical — On the Right Track

### Gate Bioscience — Oral PrP Degrader ("Molecular Gates")

- **Mechanism:** Small molecule that blocks PrP export from the ER → protein degraded intracellularly.
- **Status:** Early preclinical. $65M raised November 2025. Collaborating with Broad/Minikel lab for prion mouse survival studies.
- **Key result:** 80% brain PrP reduction in mice after 1 week of daily oral dosing.
- **v5 verdict:** Correct target — depletes PrPC substrate. Oral delivery is a major advantage over intrathecal ASO/siRNA.
- **References:** CJD Foundation drug development pipeline. Endpoints News (Gate Bioscience funding round).

### CRISPR Base Editing (Broad Institute)

- **Mechanism:** AAV-delivered base editor (BE3.9max) installs PRNP R37X premature stop codon → permanent PrP reduction. Alternative: install G127V at codon 127 for dominant-negative protection.
- **Status:** Preclinical. Engineered variants achieve 50–63% PrP reduction in mouse brain at 6.7× lower viral dose with no significant off-target editing.
- **Key result:** 52% lifespan extension in humanized prion mice.
- **v5 verdict:** Correct and potentially curative — permanent substrate removal or permanent conversion blockade.
- **References:** Nature Medicine 2024. Broad Institute news.

### Zinc Finger Repressor (Sangamo / Evotec)

- **Mechanism:** AAV-delivered zinc finger protein represses PRNP transcription. Single intravenous dose.
- **Status:** Preclinical. Long-lasting PRNP repression demonstrated in mice and nonhuman primates. Presented at ASGCT 2025.
- **v5 verdict:** Correct target — equivalent to ASO but potentially one-and-done.
- **References:** ASGCT 2025 presentations. Gene therapy news coverage.

### G127V Dominant-Negative Gene Therapy

- **Mechanism:** AAV delivers anchorless G127V PrP variant (from kuru-resistant Fore people of Papua New Guinea). Acts as dominant-negative chain terminator: incorporated into growing fibrils, destabilizes them. Works in trans. Protection persists after expression ceases. Strain-universal.
- **Status:** Preclinical 2026. PhD grant for in vivo proof-of-concept ongoing (MRC Prion Unit / UCL).
- **Key result:** scAAV encoding BvPrnpV127ΔGPI extended survival ~50 days in prion-inoculated mice.
- **v5 verdict:** Uniquely valuable — targets the LLPS liquid-to-solid transition (the gatekeeper step). Complementary to ASO: ASO removes new substrate, G127V poisons existing fibrils.
- **References:** ScienceDirect 2026. PLoS Pathog 2026. Sci Rep 2018 (NMR structure). Commun Biol 2020 (LLPS modulation). J Neurochem 2023 (condensate fluidity).

### ADAM10 / α-Cleavage Enhancers

- **Mechanism:** Increase ADAM10-mediated shedding/cleavage of PrPC. α-cleavage cuts at residue ~111, within the fibril core region, destroying the convertible substrate. C1 fragment (GPI-anchored, globular domain only) cannot be converted and may inhibit conversion.
- **Status:** Concept stage. Peptide aptamers induce α-cleavage in vitro (Corda et al., Mol Neurobiol 2018). Shed PrP antagonizes PrPSc propagation and Aβ neurotoxicity (Linsenmeier et al., Sci Adv 2021). Not in therapeutic development.
- **v5 verdict:** Correct mechanism — destroys convertible substrate. Under v5, α-cleavage protects by substrate destruction, not by releasing a neuroprotective N1 fragment. N1 transgenic mice showed no protection (Bhullar et al. 2020, PMC7253391).
- **References:** Altmeppen et al., eLife 2015;4:e04260. Linsenmeier et al., Sci Adv 2021. Bhérer et al. (C1 inhibits conversion), Prion 2012.

## Downstream Interventions — Band-aids (Won't Stop the Cascade)

### Trazodone / Dibenzoylmethane (PERK/eIF2α Pathway)

- **Mechanism:** Inhibits PERK-dependent translational repression (UPR). Restores global translation.
- **Status:** Preclinical positive. Trazodone is an approved antidepressant but not in clinical trial for prion disease. Being tested in PSP at Cambridge.
- **Key result:** Oral trazodone prevented neurodegeneration in prion-infected mice; rescued synaptic and mitochondrial proteomes.
- **v5 verdict:** Band-aid — treats a downstream branch (translational shutdown) without stopping AMP generation or membrane perturbation. Reasonable as adjunct.
- **References:** Moreno et al., Nature 2012. Halliday et al., Brain 2017;140:1768. bioRxiv 2023.

### CTEP / mGluR5 Inhibitors

- **Mechanism:** Allosteric inhibitor of metabotropic glutamate receptor 5.
- **Status:** Preclinical only.
- **Key result:** Presymptomatic CTEP: 171±4 vs 140±5 days. Post-symptomatic: zero effect. Genetic mGluR5 knockout: no survival improvement in two independent models.
- **v5 verdict:** Branch, not trunk. Narrow window (closes at symptom onset). Pharmacological > genetic effect suggests off-target contributions.
- **References:** Wang et al., Acta Neuropathol Commun 2026;14:83. Goniotaki et al., PLoS Pathog 2017.

### FK506 (Tacrolimus) — Calcineurin Inhibitor

- **Mechanism:** Activates autolysosomal degradation of abnormal PrP.
- **Status:** Preclinical. Treatment at clinical phase reduced neurodegeneration, improved behavior, increased survival.
- **v5 verdict:** Partial — has a mixed mechanism (autophagy of PrPSc + neuroprotection). May clear some toxic intermediates.
- **References:** Bhatt et al., PLoS Pathog 2010.

### NMDA Receptor Antagonists (Memantine, MK-801)

- **Mechanism:** Block NMDA-mediated Ca²⁺ influx.
- **Status:** In vitro: memantine and MK-801 prevent PrPSc-induced neuronal death in cortical cultures (Müller et al., Eur J Pharmacol 1993). In vivo: memantine rescues Prnp G92N knockin phenotype (beading, seizures, survival) (JCI 2025). **Never tested in prion-infected mice for survival — a 33-year gap.**
- **v5 verdict:** Should be tested immediately. Under v5, the PrP N-terminal AMP perturbs the membrane → NMDA senses bilayer tension (mechanosensitive) → Ca²⁺ influx. Memantine blocks this regardless of activation mechanism. FDA-approved, safe, no regulatory barrier. Expected effect: +10–19% survival alone, but critical as bridge therapy while ASO ramps up.
- **References:** Müller et al., Eur J Pharmacol 1993 (PubMed 7901042). JCI 2025 (doi:10.1172/JCI186432). Bhatt et al., Neuropathol Appl Neurobiol 2001 (NMDA binding changes in prion hippocampus).

## Failed Approaches — Why They Failed (v5 Reinterpretation)

| Treatment | Result | v5 Explanation |
|-----------|--------|----------------|
| **Quinacrine** | Zero survival benefit; generated drug-resistant prions (Ghaemmaghami et al., PLoS Pathog 2009) | Targeted PrPSc, which evolves conformationally under selective pressure. v5: target the host (PrPC), which cannot escape. |
| **Pentosan polysulfate (PPS)** | Effective in mice (early only); failed in humans (intraventricular infusion, complications) | v5 reinterpretation: PPS is a polyanion that neutralizes the cationic N-terminal AMP (not anti-conversion). Paradox resolved: PPS inhibits toxicity in cells but stimulates cell-free conversion — because cell-free has no toxicity to neutralize, and PPS's conformational effects on PrP dominate. Failed clinically due to **delivery**, not mechanism. |
| **PRN100 (anti-PrP antibody)** | 6 patients, zero efficacy signal (Lancet Neurol 2022) | Binds PrPC on the cell surface but cannot prevent conversion in the endosomal compartment where v5 says conversion primarily occurs. Surface binding ≠ conversion blockade. |
| **Doxycycline** | No consistent clinical benefit | Never had positive data in relevant preclinical models. Failure was predictable from existing data (Minikel analysis). |
| **Statins** | Failed in vivo despite cell-culture PrPSc reduction | Cannot effectively lower brain cholesterol. And removing cholesterol disrupts lipid rafts needed for normal PrPC function. |
| **D-penicillamine** (copper chelator) | +11 days in mice (modest) | Copper-PrP condensates are protective (liquid state buffers copper at synapses). Chelation destroys the protective condensates along with the pathological ones. The target should be the **oxidative trigger** (H₂O₂ → dityrosine cross-links → solid transition), not the copper itself. Evans et al., Sci Adv 2023. |
| **Bax deletion / Bcl-2 overexpression** | Zero effect on disease course | Apoptosis is a late consequence, not the proximal mechanism. Under v5, the primary damage is AMP-mediated membrane perturbation → excitotoxicity, not classical apoptosis. |
| **Flupirtine** | Marginal cognitive improvement, no survival benefit | Analgesic/neuroprotective — too far downstream. |

**Pattern across failures (Minikel analysis):** Several drugs never reduced prion accumulation in any cell assay, none had positive data in the most relevant animal models, and "the negative results were predictable from existing preclinical data." Downstream pathway interventions give 4–19% survival extension vs 2–4× for anti-propagation strategies.

## Blind Spots — v5 Predicts These Should Work, Nobody Is Testing Them

### Memantine as Bridge Therapy

- **Rationale:** FDA-approved NMDA antagonist. Under v5, the freed PrP N-terminal tail perturbs the bilayer → NMDA is mechanosensitive → Ca²⁺ → p38 MAPK → spine collapse. Memantine blocks NMDA regardless of how it was activated (glutamate or mechanical tension).
- **Evidence:** Protects neurons against PrPSc in vitro (1993). Rescues G92N knockin in vivo (JCI 2025). 33-year gap: never tested in prion-infected mice.
- **Predicted effect:** +10–19% survival alone (band-aid), but critical as temporal bridge while ASO/siRNA takes effect (2–4 weeks to ramp up). Combination ASO + memantine should outperform either alone.
- **Experiment needed:** Memantine in RML-infected mice (survival study). Trivial — drug and model both exist.

### Heparan Sulfate Analog — AMP Neutralizer

- **Rationale:** Polyanions neutralize cationic AMPs by electrostatic binding (established mechanism; heparin neutralizes LL-37 with nanomolar affinity). Heparan sulfate binds the PrP N-terminal at exactly KKRPKP: three binding sites at residues 23–52, 53–93, and 110–128 (JBC 2002). A low-MW HS analog delivered intrathecally (same route as ASO) could neutralize the freed toxic tail.
- **v5 reinterpretation of PPS:** PPS may have shown preclinical efficacy by this exact mechanism — neutralizing the AMP, not blocking conversion. The paradox (works in cells, stimulates cell-free) fits: in cells, it neutralizes toxicity; cell-free, there is no toxicity to neutralize.
- **Experiment needed:** Low-MW HS analog intrathecal in prion-infected mice. Moderate difficulty — analogous chemistry exists.
- **References:** JBC 2002 (HS binding sites on PrP). PMC10995223 (heparin neutralizes AMPs). PMC12806164 (Hayashida 2025, HS-cathelicidin). CureFFI PPS analysis.

### LLPS Modulators (Condensate-Targeted Drugs)

- **Rationale:** Small molecules that stabilize liquid PrP condensates and prevent the liquid-to-solid transition. G127V does this naturally (J Neurochem 2023). The field of "condensate-targeted drug discovery" (c-mods) is active for Alzheimer's and ALS (FASEB J 2025; Trends Pharmacol Sci 2026) but has not been applied to prion disease.
- **Status:** Zero tests in prion models despite Prion 2025 conference recognizing LLPS as a major theme.
- **Experiment needed:** Test myricetin or other c-mods in PrP LLPS assay → fibril formation assay.
- **References:** FASEB J 2025. Trends Pharmacol Sci 2026. Prion 2025 conference proceedings.

### Targeted ROS Scavenger

- **Rationale:** Evans et al. (Sci Adv 2023) showed copper-PrP condensates are protective when liquid, but Cu²⁺ + H₂O₂ triggers the liquid-to-solid transition via dityrosine cross-links. The target is the oxidative trigger, not the copper. A ROS scavenger directed to the endosomal/MVB compartment (where Cu + ROS converge and the GPI brake is released) could prevent pathological nucleation while preserving the protective copper-buffering function.
- **Available drugs:** NAC (N-acetylcysteine, approved), edaravone (approved for ALS). Neither has been tested specifically for endosomal ROS in prion context.
- **Experiment needed:** NAC or targeted antioxidant in prion-infected mice.

### Endogenous AMP Profiling

- **Rationale:** If prion neuroinflammation upregulates endogenous AMPs (LL-37/CRAMP, defensins), there is a positive feedback loop: conversion → toxicity → neuroinflammation → more AMPs → more toxicity. LL-37 is upregulated in microglia/astrocytes in AD brains (Biochem Pharmacol 2015). CRAMP (mouse cathelicidin) is highly induced during neuroinflammation (JCI 2025, PMC11785927). No one has measured AMP levels in prion-infected brain tissue.
- **Experiment needed:** Transcriptomic/proteomic profiling of AMP expression across the prion infection time course. Easy — use existing serial brain samples.

## Optimal Combination Therapy Under v5

The v5 model predicts that effective treatment requires simultaneous intervention at multiple cascade points. No single agent is sufficient post-symptomatically (except possibly high-efficacy siRNA). The proposed combination:

### Tier 1 — Kill the Source (intrathecal)

- **ASO (ION717) or siRNA (PRiSM)** — reduces PrPC substrate.
- **G127V AAV** — destabilizes existing fibrils, blocks new conversion. Strain-universal.

### Tier 2 — Neutralize the AMP (intrathecal, same route)

- **Low-MW heparan sulfate analog** — binds KKRPKP, neutralizes the freed N-terminal tail.

### Tier 3 — Protect Neurons (oral, already approved)

- **Memantine** — blocks mechanosensitive NMDA-mediated Ca²⁺ influx.
- **ROS scavenger** (NAC or targeted) — prevents Cu + H₂O₂ → solid transition.
- **Low-dose lithium** — induces autophagy via mTOR-independent pathway, neuroprotective (PubMed 30135493).

### Tier 4 — Preventive (for genetic mutation carriers)

- **CRISPR base editing** — permanently installs G127V or R37X stop.
- **LLPS modulator** (c-mod) — maintains PrP condensates in liquid state.

### Why Combination, Not Monotherapy

Anti-PrPSc drug combinations generate resistant prion strains. Berry et al. (PLoS Pathog 2020) showed that two anti-PrPSc drugs combined produced a new strain specifically resistant to the combination while remaining susceptible to either drug alone — the first pathogen to show combination-specific resistance. Alternating regimens reduced resistance but did not extend survival beyond monotherapy (J Gen Virol 2021).

The v5 combination avoids this entirely: it targets **host-encoded machinery and biophysical properties**, not the replicating pathogen. PrPC production (ASO target), NMDA channel (memantine target), and electrostatic charge (HS analog target) are all host-encoded — there is no evolutionary escape. This restores the HIV cocktail logic: attack different steps that cannot be simultaneously evaded.
